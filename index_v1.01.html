Help

Why this app works for ADHD brains (and how to use it kindly).

Core idea

Focus Compass is designed to reduce executive-function load, not to force willpower. It externalizes time and decisions, reduces overwhelm through limits, and uses gentle language to defuse shame spirals.

If you struggle with inattention, the goal is not perfect focus. It’s to make the next step so small and visible that attention has something obvious to land on.

When you feel stuck

Try a 10-minute starter, then stop. Starting is often the hardest part; a short timer is a workaround for that friction.

Use the Quick Capture box to unload thoughts. Captured tasks reduce mental noise and help you return to the moment.

If you’re spiraling, use Reset. It offers tiny, concrete actions to help your nervous system settle.

Why each feature exists

Next Step
One clear task reduces decision fatigue. It should be startable in under 10 minutes.
WIP limit (Priorities)
Limits protect attention. Fewer active items means less overwhelm and more progress.
Timers
Externalize time. Short timers create urgency without burnout and help you start.
Quick capture
Stops memory anxiety. If it’s captured, you don’t have to keep rethinking it.
Projects + Chunking
Projects are overwhelming without structure. Chunking turns them into small, doable steps.
Routines
Checklists reduce decision fatigue and keep you from reinventing the same steps.
Wins log
ADHD brains often discount progress. Logging wins rebuilds a kinder narrative.
Interruption parking lot
A safe place for thoughts that pop up mid-task so you can return to focus.
Low-stim + Nudges
Low-stim reduces visual noise; nudges reinforce self-compassion.

ADHD coping tips

Inattention
Make the first action physical and obvious: open the document, put tools on the table, or write a two-word headline.
Time blindness
Always use a timer. “Work for 10 minutes then stop” is easier than “finish it.”
Procrastination
Shrink the task until it feels almost too easy. Starting is the win.

Interrupting + long stories

Interrupting
Use “Hold that thought” during conversations. It gives your brain permission to let go without losing the idea.
Long-winded stories
Try “headline first.” Say the main point in one sentence, then ask: “Want the full story?”
Check-ins
Mid-story, pause and ask: “Is this enough detail, or should I keep going?” It builds consent and connection.

Gentle reminders

You’re not broken. You’re managing a nervous system with different wiring. Small steps are not “too small.” They’re how momentum begins.

Use this app as a support, not a scorecard. The goal is fewer spirals and more kindness, not perfect productivity.
